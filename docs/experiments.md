## Reading these numbers

- **Recall@k** and **MRR** are computed over answerable questions only; there is no evidence to retrieve for the rest.
- Evidence is matched by **page**, not chunk id, so these labels survive re-chunking. A chunk counts as a hit when its page range covers a labelled page.
- **Section acc.** is the stricter check: the right page for the right reason. A page can hold several clauses.
- Postgres full-text ranking is `ts_rank_cd`, **not BM25**. The `bm25` row is real BM25 (`rank_bm25`, in memory) so the comparison is measured rather than asserted.
- Per-question output for every row is in `evals/results/`.

## Findings

### 1. Hybrid retrieval made things worse, and the benchmark is why we know

The hybrid was the expected winner — it is what the brief's retrieval progression builds
towards. At rank 1 it lost to plain vector search (0.889 against 0.944).

The per-question output in `evals/results/` shows the mechanism. RRF discards scores and
keeps only ranks, which is what makes it robust across incomparable scales — and also
what makes it indifferent to *how much* better the top semantic match was. A chunk with
one incidental term in common gets rank 3 in the lexical arm, contributes `1/63`, and that
is enough to overtake the chunk the vector arm had ranked first. On a corpus of
paraphrased questions against good embeddings, the lexical arm is mostly contributing
noise with a vote.

### 2. The cross-encoder repairs fusion rather than improving retrieval

`hybrid + cross-encoder` scored 0.944 / 0.972 — identical to plain vector, to three
decimal places. It un-did the damage fusion caused, at 27ms instead of 16ms and a torch
dependency in the image. That is a reranker earning back the cost of the stage in front
of it, not adding value over the baseline. Both are off by default.

### 3. `ts_rank_cd` lost to real BM25, as expected, and both lost to vector

0.833 against 0.889. The gap is in the direction the theory predicts — BM25's IDF
weighting rewards rare terms, `ts_rank_cd` does not — and it is one of the few places
where this corpus produced a result consistent with its own explanation. Neither came
close to vector search, because almost every question here is a paraphrase and almost
none is a literal lookup.

That is a property of the corpus, not a finding about keyword search. Four pages contain
no UINs, no cross-references and one table; real wordings are full of them, and that is
precisely where vector-only retrieval is expected to fail.

### 4. Every configuration fails the headline question at rank 1 — and we know exactly why

`q001` — *"What is the waiting period for pre-existing diseases?"*, the single most
important question an agent asks — misses at rank 1 in **all six configurations**. The
retrieved order explains it:

```
rank 1   p1   cos 0.683   1. Definitions > 1.1 Pre-existing Disease
rank 2   p2   cos 0.629   4. Waiting Periods > 4.3 Pre-existing disease waiting period
```

The *definition* of a pre-existing disease outranks the *waiting period* for one. The two
passages share nearly all their vocabulary — "pre-existing", "disease", "policy",
"months", "insurer" — and the definition is the denser match, so cosine similarity
prefers it. The clause that actually answers the question sits at rank 2.

This is the adjacent-number trap the set was built to contain (`q001` says thirty-six
months, `q008` says forty-eight), and the system walks into it. Worse, **the hybrid pushes
the correct clause from rank 2 down to rank 3** — the clearest single illustration of why
fusion hurt here.

At k=5 both passages reach the model, which is why the live answer is correct today. But
the failure is real: in a tighter context budget, or with a document where the definition
and the clause sit further apart, the model would be handed the 48-month figure and asked
about waiting periods. That is a wrong answer with a correct-looking citation, which is
the single worst failure mode this system has.

What it argues for is not more retrieval machinery — the cross-encoder was given both
passages and still preferred the definition. It argues for **section-aware retrieval**:
a question about a waiting period should prefer chunks whose `section_path` contains
"Waiting Period". The metadata to do that is already on every chunk and currently unused
at query time. That is the first thing to try in the next phase.

### 5. The one false abstention is a labelling flaw, not a system failure

Every configuration shows a 6% false-abstention rate, and it is the same question each
time: `q004` — *"Does this policy cover a surgery after six months?"*, which is the
brief's **own example question**.

The system abstains. Reading the document, that is defensible: the answer genuinely
depends on whether the condition is pre-existing (36 months), listed (24 months) or
neither (30 days), and the document cannot resolve which without facts the question does
not supply. The ideal response is "it depends, and here are the three waiting periods" —
which is neither a clean answer nor a clean abstention.

`answerable` is a boolean, so the label cannot express that, and a defensible response is
being counted as an error. The honest conclusion is that the *schema* is wrong, not the
system: the harness needs a third state for questions whose correct answer is conditional,
scored on whether the conditions were surfaced rather than on answer/abstain. Until then,
read the 6% as one known mislabel rather than a measured failure.

That the brief's own worked example is the question that breaks the labelling scheme is
worth noticing.

### 6. Abstention behaviour is identical across all six configurations

0% false answers and the same single false abstention, whatever ranked the candidates.
That is the score-scale separation doing its job: the confidence signal is a cosine
similarity in every mode, so changing the ranking strategy cannot move the abstention
boundary. Before the fix, switching to hybrid would have driven abstention to 100%.

### 7. Latency differences at this scale are noise

Vector shows 16ms against hybrid's 5ms, which cannot be true — the hybrid does strictly
more work. Vector runs first in the matrix and pays the cold-cache cost. At single-digit
milliseconds over 8 chunks, nothing here is a latency measurement worth acting on. The
numbers start to mean something at a few thousand chunks.

## What this table cannot tell you

**The corpus is too small and too clean for any of this to generalise.** 18 answerable
questions over one synthetic 4-page document that we wrote ourselves. Recall@5 was 1.000
for every configuration — the cut-off had to drop to k=1 before the table discriminated
at all, and even then the MRR spread across six configurations is 0.055.

Specifically, this corpus is missing everything that makes retrieval hard:

- **Scale.** 8 chunks. Retrieving 5 of 8 is not retrieval, it is enumeration.
- **Distractors.** Real wordings repeat the same concept across definitions, exclusions,
  schedules and endorsements. Here each fact appears once, so there is nothing to
  confuse.
- **Structural mess.** This fixture has clean headings and one ruled table because a
  script drew it. Real wordings have scanned pages, multi-column layouts and nested
  numbering.
- **Literal lookups.** The case the keyword arm exists for barely occurs here.
- **Version conflicts.** One document, one version. The brief names version mixing as a
  main risk and this set cannot exercise it at all.
- **A third answer state.** `answerable` is a boolean; finding 5 shows that is not enough.

So the honest reading is: *vector-only retrieval is sufficient on an easy corpus, and the
added machinery has not yet earned its place.* Not: *hybrid retrieval does not help.*

The two concrete next steps, in order: **section-aware retrieval** (finding 4 — the
metadata is already there), and a **third answerability state** in the harness
(finding 5). Then real insurer policy wordings — ideally two versions of one
product, so version-conflict behaviour becomes testable. The harness, the metrics and the
whole matrix will re-run against them unchanged; only `evals/questions.yaml` grows.
